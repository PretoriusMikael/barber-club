# Barber Club — what you are looking at, and what we still need

**Companion notes to the new website preview.** *Last updated 27 August 2026.*

Read this alongside the preview link. It covers three things:

1. **What was taken out** before we sent it — working notes that belonged to the build, not to the finished site.
2. **The photography and video we need**, which is the single thing standing between this preview and a launch.
3. **The information only you can give us**, with the reason each one matters.

Nothing on the preview is invented. Every price, address, phone number, opening time and line of brand copy is taken verbatim from barberclub.co.za. Where the current site does not answer a question, the new one stays quiet rather than guessing — which is why some of the asks below exist.

## 2. Photography and video — the critical path

This is the honest headline: **the site is built and the content is not shot.** Everything else on this list is a phone call or an email. This one is a diary date, and it is the item most often underestimated.

> A technically flawless site with weak photography will underperform a mediocre site with excellent photography, every time.

**A limitation worth knowing.** Four of these are 456 × 532 pixels and the group shot is 950 × 689. That is fine at the size we show them and nowhere near enough for anything full-width — which is why the team photograph is shown in a contained frame rather than edge to edge. **If the original camera files exist, please send those**; we would rather crop down from a full-size original than scale a small file up.

### 2.2 The shoot we recommend

- **Half a day of stills, plus half a day of video, across at least three branches.** An eleven-branch group photographed entirely in one shop looks like one shop.
- **Cast for range.** Different hair textures and different skin tones. A Cape Winelands barber gallery that shows no textured or coily work quietly tells a large part of the market that it is not for them.
- **Shoot the room, not only the haircut.** Your own copy sells coffee, music and Wi-Fi. Photograph those.
- **Signed releases** for anyone recognisable, and **parental consent in writing** for every child.
- **Refresh the gallery monthly** from phone shots; re-shoot properly every six months.

### 2.4 Technical specs for the photographer

- **Deliver full-resolution originals**, minimum 3000px on the long edge. We handle compression and format conversion.
- **Ratios:** 4:5 for service and portrait shots, 3:2 or 16:9 for the wide bands, 3:4 for barber portraits.
- **Colour:** warm and consistent. The site is near-black, so frames with a dark or controlled background sit best.
- **Keep the raw files.** Site formats change; camera originals do not.

**Total outstanding: 26 stills, 11 shopfronts, one portrait per barber, one video loop.**

## 3. What we need from you

Each of these is a real gap on the current barberclub.co.za, not an oversight on ours. Nothing has been invented to cover them.

| # | What we need | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **3.1** | **Barber roster** — name, home branch, speciality, years behind the chair, and one sentence in their own words | *"Book with [name]"* is the strongest call to action a barber site has for returning customers. Your current site sells "a legendary team of experienced barbers" across 17 pages and names none of them. Please collect the sentence from each barber rather than writing it for them |
| **3.2** | **Which branches are Classic and which are Premier** | The two tiers are described everywhere and mapped nowhere. This decides the badge on every branch card, and where a Premier booking is routed |

## 4. Booking — the biggest single opportunity

**There is no online booking anywhere on the current site**, while Premier is advertised as *"by appointment only"* and the only mechanism offered across all 17 pages is a phone number. Every enquiry outside trading hours, across eleven branches, is lost.

The new site is already built for a booking system: every "Book" button on every page routes through one place in the code, so switching it on is a configuration change, not a rebuild. Until then, `/book` gives customers a real working path — the branch's phone number — rather than a "coming soon" box.

**Choosing a provider is your decision, and it is the gating one.** Score candidates on these, in this order:

- [ ] **Multiple locations, properly supported** — non-negotiable at eleven branches. Customers must not be able to book the wrong town
- [ ] Confirmed availability and support in South Africa
- [ ] **Links straight to a named barber**, so "Book with [name]" works
- [ ] Rand pricing and a local payment gateway if you ever want deposits (Payfast, Yoco, Peach)
- [ ] **Automated WhatsApp or SMS reminders** — the single biggest lever on no-shows
- [ ] **Reserve with Google** — captures people who never reach the website at all
- [ ] Reports a completed booking back to your analytics, so we can see what the site is actually earning

Worth quoting: **Fresha, Setmore, Acuity, Timely, Booksy.** Confirm South African coverage and current pricing with each directly. Squire is US-focused and likely a poor fit.

## 5. The legal pages are proposed, not final

`/legal/privacy` (POPIA) and `/legal/terms` (booking, cancellation, no-shows) are written out in full so you can read them as they would appear. **They are drafts, and they are not legal advice.**

Your current site publishes neither, so there was nothing to transcribe. What is on the preview is a set of standard defaults — a four-hour cancellation window, a seven-day put-it-right guarantee, five-year record retention, a thirty-day response window on data requests. **Every one is a proposal for you to accept, change or reject.**

Before either page goes live:

1. **Confirm every clause**, especially the cancellation window — it must match whatever the booking system enforces, or it turns into a customer complaint.
2. **Supply the registered company name** and **appoint an Information Officer**, registered with the Information Regulator. POPIA requires both.
3. **Have the privacy notice reviewed by someone qualified**, and check it against what your booking provider actually does with customer data — they are processing it on your behalf.

## 6. What is already working on the preview

So the list above reads in proportion — this is finished and live in the demo:

- **All eleven branches**, each with its own page, full address, own phone number, per-day opening hours, public-holiday hours, a live "open now / closed" badge, directions, and search markup that tells Google each is a real local business.
- **The full service menu, both tiers, on one page** with a Classic ↔ Premier switch. Your current site splits them across two unlinked pages, so nobody can compare R290 against R390 without opening two tabs.
- **The group packages surfaced** — Johnnie Red, Black and Blue — with their own page and a block on the home page. At R450–R650 a head against R160 for a walk-in, this is the highest-value thing you sell and today it is buried on a sub-menu.
- **A booking path from anywhere**: header, hero, a thumb-reach bar on mobile, and the end of every section.
- **Built for phones first**, on the assumption of mobile data — because that is how almost everyone will arrive.
- **Link previews and a browser icon**, so a link shared in a WhatsApp group looks like Barber Club rather than a grey rectangle.
- **A cookie-safe map** on every branch page: it loads only when a visitor asks for it, which keeps the page fast and keeps the privacy notice truthful.
- **Accessible throughout** — completable by keyboard, readable by a screen reader, and contrast-checked against the near-black palette.
